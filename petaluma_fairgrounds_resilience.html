Energy Disruption & Grid Vulnerability

Highly Likely (<0.5 days) | Likely (PSPS)

The property at 175 Fairgrounds Drive operates within PG&E's North Bay distribution territory. Energy disruptions stem from two principal vectors: localized distribution failures during winter storms/heat waves (<0.5 days) and utility Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS) triggered during severe Diablo wind events and low-humidity fire weather.

Short-Term Outages (<0.5 Days)

Highly Likely (1–10 Years)

Nuisance and heat-wave transformer overloads occur annually to semi-annually. Typical duration: 2 to 8 hours.

Long-Term / PSPS Outages (>24 Hours)

Likely (11–50 Years / Recurring)

PSPS events shut down transmission/distribution circuits feeding Petaluma during high wind/dry foliage events (e.g., 2019 Kincade fire shutoffs lasted 48–72 hours).

Critical Infrastructure Requirement:

The planned $72M Public Safety Facility and Community Resilience Center (CRC) at the site require heavy-duty dual-fuel emergency generators, microgrid capabilities, and battery energy storage systems (BESS) to maintain 100% operational continuity during multi-day regional blackout events.

Habitat Loss & Ecosystem Health

Likely (Urbanization & Climate Pressures)

The 55-acre campus represents an altered urban landscape adjacent to vital ecological corridors, including Kenilworth Park, Washington Creek, and the tidal salt marshes of the lower Petaluma River.

Ecosystem & Wildlife Impact

Loss of native oak savannah canopy and riparian buffer zones. Master plan includes bioswales, native planting, and restoration connecting to the 67-acre Petaluma River Park project downstream.

Food System Disruption

Preserving the Sonoma-Marin Fair agricultural exhibits and regional Farmer's Market supports local food security and agricultural heritage against suburban sprawl.
